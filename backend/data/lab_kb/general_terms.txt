Lipids are fats in the blood that help support normal body functions, and a typical lipid profile measures HDL, LDL, triglycerides, and total cholesterol. HDL is often considered the “good” cholesterol because it helps clear excess cholesterol from the bloodstream, while LDL is sometimes labeled the “bad” cholesterol since higher levels may contribute to plaque buildup in arteries. Triglycerides reflect another form of circulating fat used for energy, and elevated values may be influenced by diet, lifestyle, or metabolic conditions. Reference ranges differ across laboratories, and what is ideal depends on overall health and risk factors. It is always best to review your specific lipid values with your doctor to understand what they mean for you.
